Revision: Vectors

Key Concept: A vector is a quantity that has both magnitude (size) and direction.

Before we dive into Newton's Laws of Motion, it's crucial to understand vectors, as forces are vector quantities.

Vector vs Scalar

  • Vector: Has both magnitude and direction (e.g., Force, velocity, displacement)
  • Scalar: Has only magnitude (e.g., Mass, time, temperature)

Examples

Vectors: 5 m/s east, 10 N downward, 3 m north

Scalars: 5 seconds, 10 kilograms, 35°C

Vector Notation

We represent vectors in different ways:

  • Bold letter: F
  • Letter with arrow: $\vec{F}$
  • Components: $F_x$ and $F_y$

Vector Operations

Vector Addition

To add vectors, we add their components:

$\vec{C} = \vec{A} + \vec{B}$

$C_x = A_x + B_x$

$C_y = A_y + B_y$

Vector Subtraction

$\vec{D} = \vec{A} - \vec{B}$

$D_x = A_x - B_x$

$D_y = A_y - B_y$

Multiplication by a Scalar

$\vec{E} = k\vec{A}$

$E_x = kA_x$

$E_y = kA_y$

Vector Components

Any vector can be broken down into x and y components:

For a vector $\vec{F}$ with magnitude F and angle θ:

$F_x = F\cos\theta$

$F_y = F\sin\theta$

The magnitude of the vector from components:

$|\vec{F}| = \sqrt{F_x^2 + F_y^2}$

Direction (angle): $\theta = \tan^{-1}(F_y/F_x)$

Remember

When solving physics problems involving forces, always pay attention to directions and use vector components to find resultant effects.

What is Force?

Key Concept: Force is a push or pull that can cause an object to accelerate.

Force is a fundamental concept in physics and is essential to understanding how objects move and interact.

Definition

A force is a vector quantity that causes:

  • A stationary object to move
  • A moving object to change speed
  • A moving object to change direction
  • An object to deform (change shape)

SI Unit of Force

The SI unit of force is the newton (N)

1 newton is the force needed to give a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s²

1 N = 1 kg·m/s²

Characteristics of Force

  • Vector quantity: Has both magnitude and direction
  • Contact vs Non-contact: Some forces require physical contact, others act at a distance
  • Balanced vs Unbalanced: Determines whether an object accelerates

Important Note

Force is not the same as energy or momentum. Force causes changes in momentum according to Newton's laws.
